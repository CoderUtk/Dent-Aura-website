Choosing between braces and aligners

All orthodontic treatment works the same way: gentle, sustained pressure moves a tooth through bone, and the bone remodels behind it. What differs is how that pressure is applied, how visible the appliance is, and how much of the outcome depends on you remembering to wear it.

Metal braces

Brackets bonded to the teeth with a wire running through them. They handle every kind of movement, including the difficult ones — rotating a tooth, moving a root, closing large gaps after extractions. They're the most affordable option and the most predictable, because they're working 24 hours a day whether or not you're thinking about them. The trade-off is visibility, and needing to be careful with hard and sticky food.

Ceramic braces

The same system with tooth-coloured brackets. Much less noticeable at conversational distance, somewhat more expensive, and slightly bulkier. The brackets can pick up staining from tea, coffee and turmeric if cleaning slips.

Clear aligners

A series of removable transparent trays, each moving the teeth a fraction of a millimetre before you change to the next. They're nearly invisible, you take them out to eat, and cleaning your teeth stays normal. The catch is real: they only work while they're in your mouth, and that needs to be 20 to 22 hours a day. Aligners also handle some movements less predictably than fixed braces, so they aren't the answer for every case.

If you know you'll struggle to wear something consistently for 22 hours a day, fixed braces will usually give you a better result for less money. It's worth being honest with yourself about that at the start rather than a year in.

Why straightening teeth is more than cosmetic

  • Crowded and overlapping teeth trap plaque in places a brush can't reach, which drives decay and gum disease
  • Teeth that meet unevenly wear each other down and can overload individual teeth
  • A deep bite can cause the lower teeth to bite into the gum behind the upper front teeth
  • Protruding front teeth are considerably more likely to be broken in a fall or knock

That said, wanting straighter teeth purely because you'd like to smile without thinking about it is a perfectly good reason on its own.

What treatment involves

  1. Assessment. Photos, X-rays and impressions or a scan, so we can see the roots and the way your jaws relate to each other — not just the teeth.
  2. Plan and estimate. What can realistically be achieved, roughly how long it will take, whether extractions are needed, and the cost.
  3. Preparation. Any decay, gum disease or teeth needing root canal treatment are dealt with first. Moving teeth in an unhealthy mouth makes things worse.
  4. Fitting. Braces are bonded on in one appointment, usually one to two hours. Aligners are issued as a set with instructions.
  5. Adjustments. Roughly every four to eight weeks for braces; aligner reviews are usually less frequent.
  6. Retainers. Fitted as soon as the braces come off.

How long does it take?

Most comprehensive cases run 12 to 24 months. Minor crowding of the front teeth alone can sometimes be corrected in six to nine months. Anyone quoting you a firm duration before looking at your X-rays is guessing.

Retainers are not optional

Teeth have a persistent tendency to drift back toward where they started, and that tendency never fully goes away. Retainers — either a thin wire bonded behind the front teeth, a removable clear retainer worn at night, or both — are what hold the result. Stopping retainer wear after a year or two is the single most common reason people end up with crooked teeth again after paying for treatment.

Common questions

Am I too old for braces?

No. Teeth move through bone at any age, and adults make up a large share of orthodontic patients. The biology is slightly slower in adults and gum health needs closer attention, but the treatment works. What matters far more than age is whether your gums and the bone supporting your teeth are healthy.

Do braces hurt?

Fitting them doesn't hurt. For the first three to five days afterwards, and for a day or two after each adjustment, the teeth feel tender and biting is uncomfortable — soft food and ordinary painkillers cover it. The brackets can also rub the cheeks at first until the tissue toughens up; orthodontic wax helps during that period.

Will I need teeth removed?

Sometimes. Where there is severe crowding or the front teeth protrude significantly, there may not be room to align everything without creating space. Modern treatment avoids extractions where it reasonably can, using expansion or slight reshaping between teeth instead. It's decided from your X-rays and models, not as a routine.

What can't I eat with braces?

Avoid hard things that can snap a bracket off — ice, hard nuts, bones, hard boiled sweets — and sticky things that pull at the wire, like chewing gum and toffee. Cut apples, carrots and corn off the cob rather than biting into them. With clear aligners this doesn't apply, because you take them out to eat.

How do I clean my teeth with braces on?

Brush after every meal, angling the brush both above and below the brackets, and use interdental brushes to get under the wire. This part genuinely matters: plaque left around brackets causes permanent white marks on the enamel that are still there after the braces come off.
